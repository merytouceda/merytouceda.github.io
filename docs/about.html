About me

I’m a postdoctoral researcher at the Bioinformatics Research Center at North Carolina State University, where I study viral, bacterial and plasmid communities in environmental metagenomes. I’m interested in how microbes adapt to a changing climate, and in finding solutions that improve outcomes for people and the planet.

I care about open science, reproducibility and FAIR data: good science should be usable and verifiable by others, not just publishable. As a Nextflow Ambassador (2026), I lead workshops and write training materials that take graduate students and professionals from zero to running their own pipelines.

Research interests

Viruses and plasmids in the environment

Viral and mobile genetic element discovery in soil, wastewater and gut metagenomes, and what these elements tell us about how microbial communities respond to disturbance.

Reproducible, benchmarked pipelines

Nextflow pipelines that benchmark machine-learning viral inference tools against simulated communities with known ground truth, to find out where they fail.

Open science and training

Bioinformatics curricula, from HPC and Bash to metagenomics and Nextflow, built to make computational biology accessible to non-specialists.
